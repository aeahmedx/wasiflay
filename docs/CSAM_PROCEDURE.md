# Child sexual abuse material — required procedure

Internal. Not published in the app.

US law requires providers to report apparent child sexual abuse material
to the NCMEC CyberTipline when they become aware of it. This procedure
exists so that happens correctly and so nobody makes the situation worse
by improvising.

**Designated responsible person: Ahmed Ahmed — wasiflay@gmail.com**

---

## If you are a moderator and you see it

1. **Remove it.** Use Remove, not Delete permanently. This hides it from
   everyone while preserving it.
2. **Suspend the account.**
3. **Contact Ahmed immediately.** Use the word "urgent".
4. **Stop there.**

**Do not:**
- Download it, screenshot it, or save it anywhere
- Forward it to anyone, including other moderators
- Reply to the post or contact the person who posted it
- Delete it permanently — that destroys what has to be preserved

Handling the file yourself can itself be a criminal offence. Removing it
from view and escalating is the complete extent of your job.

---

## If you are Ahmed

**1. Preserve.** Do not permanently delete anything — not the content,
the account, the messages, or the uploaded file. Take note of:
- The account's user ID, display name, and email
- Timestamps of the content
- The storage path of any image
- Any other accounts that interacted with it

**2. Report to NCMEC** at report.cybertip.org, or call 1-800-843-5678.
Reporting is a legal obligation, not a judgement call, and it applies
even if you are unsure. Report anyway — assessing it is their job.

**3. Preserve for 90 days** after reporting, as the law requires, and
follow any instructions NCMEC or law enforcement give you.

**4. Terminate the account.** Suspend rather than delete, so the record
survives.

**5. Check for reach.** If a minor was contacted, look at whether other
minors were approached by the same account.

**6. Write it down.** Date, what was found, what was reported, the NCMEC
report number, and what was done. Keep it somewhere private and durable.

---

## If a minor reports being approached

1. Believe them and act immediately — suspend the adult's account first,
   ask questions after.
2. Do not ask the minor for details of what was sent, and do not ask them
   to forward anything.
3. Preserve everything.
4. Report to NCMEC.
5. If a parent or guardian can be reached, tell them.

---

## Design choices that support this

These already exist in the platform and should not be weakened:

- **No direct messages.** Every conversation is public and moderatable.
  The single most common grooming pattern needs a private channel, and
  there isn't one. Do not add DMs without revisiting this document.
- **Under-18 accounts never expose location publicly**, enforced by a
  database trigger rather than by interface code.
- **Under-18 contact details are never released to event organisers.**
- **Age is recorded at signup** and cannot be edited by the account
  holder.
- **Removal is reversible; deletion is admin-only.** Moderators cannot
  accidentally destroy evidence.
